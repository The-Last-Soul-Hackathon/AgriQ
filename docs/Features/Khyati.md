1. Net Realization Engine

What it does: Shows the farmer what money they'll actually end up with — not just the price someone offers.

Example: Buyer A offers ₹30/kg but is far away (transport costs ₹8, extra fees ₹1) — farmer really keeps only ₹21/kg. Buyer B offers just ₹26/kg but is nearby (only ₹1 transport, ₹0.5 fees) — farmer keeps ₹24.5/kg. The engine tells the farmer: "Buyer B is your better deal," even though the price looked smaller at first.

2. Escrow + Milestone-Based Settlement

What it does: Locks the buyer's payment in a safe "middle box" and releases it to the farmer only after the crop is delivered and confirmed.

Example: Like paying for something online where your money isn't given to the seller immediately — it's held by the platform (like Amazon holds payment) and only released once you receive your order and confirm it's correct. Farmer never has to worry, "What if I send my crop and never get paid?"

3. Auto-Aggregation for Non-FPO Farmers

What it does: Combines small quantities from many farmers into one big lot so they can sell to big buyers who don't want to deal with tiny amounts.

Example: A wholesaler wants 10 tonnes of tomatoes but won't talk to a farmer who only has 1 tonne. Our platform combines Farmer A's 1 tonne + Farmer B's 2 tonnes + Farmer C's 3 tonnes + Farmer D's 4 tonnes = 10 tonnes, and now all four farmers together can supply that one big buyer and get a better price than selling alone.

4. Portable QR-Linked Quality Record

What it does: Attaches a QR code to the crop that stores photos, quality, and weight — as proof — right from the farm to the final buyer.

Example: Like a courier tracking number and delivery receipt. If a buyer later says "the crop wasn't as good as promised," the farmer can point to the QR record — showing the photo and grade recorded at pickup — as proof, instead of it becoming a "he said, she said" argument.

5. Offline-First Access (IVR/SMS)

What it does: Lets farmers use the platform through a basic phone call or text message — no smartphone or internet required.

Example: Like how you can check your bank balance by giving a "missed call" without any app — a farmer can call a number, press 1 to hear today's crop price in Hindi, press 2 to book a mandi slot, all from a basic ₹500 keypad phone.
